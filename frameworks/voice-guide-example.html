1. Our voice in a nutshell

Acme Deals' voice personified is a 'Fellow Operator with a dealmaker's grin'.

Our copy reads like another agency owner showing you the maths on their own stack: money first, in the customer's own words. It never hypes and never talks about itself.

4. Writing principles

Cadence and sentence length

  • Average sentence length: 6.4 words, across 29 sentences in on-brand/ads.md.
  • Longest sentence: 13 words: "The lifetime deals that let a one-person shop look like a full team."
  • How paragraphs open: on a verb, most often: Own, Stop, Buy, Cut, Run, Look. Never on the brand name.
  • Rhythm: 11 of 29 sentences are four words or fewer. Fragments are fine ("No seats. No renewals."). Use triplets of short clauses for the money mechanics ("Own it once, bill it into retainers, keep the margin."). No questions in the samples, and no "we" or "our".

Always

  • Lead with the outcome or the job to be done, not the product name (guides/voice-rules.md).
  • Put a number, a condition, or a customer quote in every claim.
  • Use the customer's word when there is one, grammar intact: "lifetime deals", "cheat code", "punch above your weight", "overhead", "retainer", "margin" (guides/voice-rules.md).
  • Read it out loud. If it sounds like a brand communicating, rewrite it (guides/voice-rules.md).

Never

  • Em dashes, hashtags in body copy, or emoji (guides/voice-rules.md).
  • The hype words and banned phrases in section 5.
  • More than one idea per asset (guides/voice-rules.md; off-brand #7).
  • Speed promises with no proof: "in just minutes" (off-brand #5).

5. Language and terminology

Jargon our buyers use stays: retainer, white-label, seats, stack. Buzzwords go: if a word falls apart when you ask "how, exactly?", cut it. Customer words win where they differ from ours.

6. Branded language: the Acme Deals / cheat code element

Overview: visual and verbal consistency

  • Product and owned terms: Acme Deals (always two words, both capitalised). Owned key message: "Buy once, bill clients monthly" (positioning-messaging.md).
  • Signature element: the agency cheat code. A customer's phrase ("lifetime deals are a cheat code for an agency", Jordan Reyes, reviews.md and customers.csv) adopted as a 5/5 headline ("The agency cheat code", on-brand/ads.md). It frames buying a deal as a smart operator's move, not a discount.
  • Visual: a bold typographic card with a red accent (on-brand/ads.md, image assets). The wordmark is horizontal with the same red accent.

Practical application

  • Where it appears: headlines, image overlays, and the opening line of a campaign. Once per asset at most.
  • Where it never appears: support replies, invoices, and anything about licensing terms inferred These need precision, not a wink.
  • How to write it: "the agency cheat code", lowercase in running text, never "Cheat Code™", and never with "secret" or "hack" next to it (off-brand #8).
